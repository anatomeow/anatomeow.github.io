Digestive System

The digestive system is responsible for breaking down the food we eat into nutrients that can be absorbed and utilized by the body.

Overview

Stomach Structure

The stomach is a J-shaped muscular organ located in the upper left abdomen, serving as a temporary storage and mixing chamber for food. It consists of four main regions: the cardia (connects to esophagus), fundus (upper dome), body (main central region), and pylorus (connects to small intestine). The stomach wall has four layers: mucosa (innermost secretory lining), submucosa (connective tissue with blood vessels), muscularis (three layers of smooth muscle for churning), and serosa (outer protective covering). The stomach can expand to hold 1-1.5 liters of food and fluid. Its highly folded inner lining (rugae) allows for expansion and increases surface area for secretion and absorption.
